# Content and scope standards

Target learners: EMT, Advanced EMT, Paramedic, and Critical Care Paramedic (specialty study track). New clinical content and edits should identify the minimum learner level, sources, and reference-check date. The 10-minute flow uses an explicitly screened bank rather than inferring scope from a topic name.

Scope framework: NASEMSO/NHTSA, *National EMS Scope of Practice Model 2019: Including Change Notices 1.0 and 2.0*, DOT HS 813 151 (August 2021): https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/56917 . Skills tables: document pages 25–32 (PDF pages 39–46). The model guides education; state law, credentialing, and medical direction determine authorization.

AEMT scope examples in the current bank: supraglottic airway placement and peripheral IV initiation. Electrical cardioversion, manual defibrillation, ECG interpretation, and endotracheal intubation are not presented as EMT/AEMT procedures. Recognition, BLS support, and requests for higher-level care may be taught at these levels.

The national model defines EMR, EMT, AEMT, and Paramedic; Critical Care Paramedic is not a fifth national scope level. Critical-care content builds on the paramedic framework and does not imply authorization for transport ventilators, infusions, or other specialty procedures. State rules, employer credentialing, training, transport protocols, and medical direction govern practice.

Clinical hierarchy:
- AHA 2025 peer-reviewed BLS/ACLS/PALS guidelines for resuscitation; ALS recommendations must be translated into the learner's authorized actions.
- NAEMT PHTLS and GEMS topic frameworks for trauma and older-adult assessment. Public course pages describe the frameworks, not the full clinical curricula. Do not claim NAEMT endorsement or copy proprietary course questions.
- Accessible peer-reviewed clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, and original studies for specific claims; distinguish their evidence strength from the national consensus scope model.

Each quick-study item stores its scope link, clinical source, minimum level, and checked date. Short sessions use four screened BLS/pediatric challenge families and two original advanced challenge families. Paramedic and CCP sessions reserve at least two questions and two distinct recall cards for their selected track; lower-level material fills the remaining slots, prioritized by confidence errors and review needs. The larger legacy question and flashcard banks have not yet received a complete item-by-item scope/source audit and are not automatically included in the new flow.
