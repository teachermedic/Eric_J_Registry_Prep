Hemoglobin → Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

Follow impaired oxygen carriage and cellular toxicity to brain and heart injury.

Hemoglobin transports oxygen in blood. Carbon monoxide (CO) interferes with oxygen carriage and unloading, and also affects cellular respiration. A familiar oxygen-saturation number may therefore be misleading.

Start with this patient

Several people in one building develop headache and nausea while a fuel-burning appliance is running. One becomes confused. Consider a shared exposure and scene safety before entering or treating.

Where would you like to start?

EMT: three simple stops. AEMT: six mechanism connections. Paramedic: deeper physiology and treatment reasoning. Learning views do not expand clinical scope; care follows local protocols and medical direction.

Hemoglobin transports oxygen

Hemoglobin transports oxygen

EMT · Recognize

Hemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen from the lungs to tissues.

AEMT · Connect

Oxygen bound to hemoglobin supplies most arterial oxygen content. Dissolved oxygen contributes a smaller portion under ordinary conditions.

Paramedic · Explain

Oxygen delivery depends on hemoglobin concentration, its oxygen loading and unloading, and blood flow. Oxygen tension and oxygen content describe different properties.

Evidence: [1], [5]

What carries most oxygen in normal arterial blood?

Explain the connection

Gas tension is not the same as total oxygen content.

CO competes for binding

CO competes for binding

EMT · Recognize

Carbon monoxide binds hemoglobin and interferes with its oxygen job.

AEMT · Connect

CO occupies binding sites and impairs release of oxygen from remaining oxygenated sites. Tissue delivery can fall even when lungs contain oxygen.

Paramedic · Explain

Carboxyhemoglobin reduces available oxygen-binding capacity and shifts the remaining oxygen-binding relationship toward tighter retention. Arterial oxygen tension can remain normal despite impaired carriage.

Evidence: [1], [5]

Can normal oxygen tension prove normal oxygen delivery in CO exposure?

Explain the connection

A normal PaO2 is not proof of adequate oxygen content or unloading.

Delivery and respiration fail

Delivery and respiration fail

EMT · Recognize

CO can reduce oxygen delivery and disrupt cellular energy use, causing brain or heart dysfunction.

AEMT · Connect

CO toxicity extends beyond hemoglobin. Interactions with cellular proteins can impair respiration and contribute to oxidative injury.

Paramedic · Explain

Binding at cytochrome c oxidase can interfere with the mitochondrial electron transport chain. Inflammatory and oxidative pathways contribute to injury, so COHb alone does not summarize the entire toxic effect.

Evidence: [1], [2]

Is CO poisoning only a problem of occupied hemoglobin sites?

Explain the connection

The blood-carriage effect is central but not the whole disease.

A saturation can mislead

A saturation can mislead

EMT · Recognize

A conventional pulse oximeter may appear reassuring despite CO poisoning.

AEMT · Connect

Conventional two-wavelength oximetry does not adequately distinguish oxyhemoglobin from carboxyhemoglobin. History and the clinical pattern remain essential.

Paramedic · Explain

Blood co-oximetry measures hemoglobin species. A conventional SpO2 or normal PaO2 cannot rule out exposure; interpretation of COHb also depends on elapsed time and prior oxygen treatment.

Evidence: [3], [5]

Does normal conventional SpO2 rule out CO poisoning?

Explain the connection

Use the exposure history and appropriate diagnostic testing.

Recognize the shared exposure

Recognize the shared exposure

EMT · Recognize

Headache, nausea, confusion, or chest symptoms in several people can suggest a shared CO exposure.

AEMT · Connect

Assess symptoms, potential combustion sources, duration, pregnancy, and cardiac or neurological involvement. Protect responders and remove patients only through a safe rescue process.

Paramedic · Explain

Brain and heart dysfunction indicate important severity even when the measured COHb is lower than expected. Exposure may have ended or oxygen treatment begun before sampling.

Evidence: [3], [4]

What matters beyond a COHb value?

Explain the connection

The clinical pattern and sampling context shape interpretation.

Patient findings → oxygen and evaluation

Patient findings → oxygen and evaluation

EMT · Recognize

Suspected CO exposure needs a safe scene, oxygen treatment, and urgent evaluation.

AEMT · Connect

Provide high-concentration oxygen and support ventilation under protocol. Notify the receiving team about the exposure, neurological or cardiac findings, and pregnancy when relevant.

Paramedic · Explain

Oxygen speeds CO elimination and supports oxygenation, while hospital assessment determines organ injury and specialist treatment needs. Improvement does not rule out delayed neurological complications.

Evidence: [3], [4], [5]

Should oxygen be withheld because conventional SpO2 is normal?

Why can normal PaO2 and conventional SpO2 coexist with serious CO poisoning?

Explain the connection

Do not use conventional saturation as a reason to withhold indicated CO treatment.

Oxygen tension, conventional saturation, hemoglobin species, and tissue utilization measure different things. Treat the exposure and clinical pattern.

Treatment: connect the why

Prehospital actions require authorization. Definitive and long-term care are included to explain physiology, not to expand field scope.

EMT · Recognize

Prioritize scene safety and a protected rescue. Provide high-concentration oxygen and ventilation support as indicated by local protocol. Arrange evaluation and communicate exposure details and the number of potentially affected people.

Evidence: [1], [3], [4], [5]

AEMT · Connect

Oxygen increases the gradient for CO elimination and supports tissue oxygen availability. Conventional saturation does not guide exclusion of poisoning. Reassess neurological function, chest symptoms, breathing, and perfusion.

Evidence: [1], [3], [4], [5]

Paramedic · Explain

Prehospital: provide high-concentration oxygen without waiting for conventional SpO2 to fall, and support ventilation if inadequate. The goal is faster displacement and elimination of CO plus support of oxygen delivery. Monitor for dysrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, altered consciousness, and other complications. A normal PaO2 reflects dissolved oxygen, not intact hemoglobin transport; normal conventional saturation can also mislead.

Definitive care: blood co-oximetry and organ assessment help characterize the exposure, but COHb can fall with time and oxygen and does not reliably encode injury severity. Hyperbaric oxygen may be considered through urgent specialist consultation for selected patients, including serious neurological or cardiac involvement and pregnancy-related risk. Its additional long-term benefit is debated across studies; it should not be presented as an automatic treatment for every exposure or as proven by one cutoff.

Continue serial neurological and cardiovascular assessment and provide the receiving team with exposure duration, treatment timing, and observed findings. Initial recovery does not exclude delayed neurological effects, so hospital guidance and follow-up matter.

Evidence: [1], [3], [4], [5]

Keep the whole picture

Do not enter an unsafe atmosphere without appropriate protection and scene clearance. Normal conventional pulse oximetry does not exclude CO poisoning. COHb concentration alone does not reliably predict the full clinical severity.
