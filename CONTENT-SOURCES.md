# Content and scope standards

Target learners: EMT, Advanced EMT, Paramedic, and Critical Care Paramedic (specialty study track). New clinical content and edits should identify the minimum learner level, sources, and reference-check date. The 10-minute flow uses an explicitly screened bank rather than inferring scope from a topic name.

Scope framework: NASEMSO/NHTSA, *National EMS Scope of Practice Model 2019: Including Change Notices 1.0 and 2.0*, DOT HS 813 151 (August 2021): https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/56917 . Skills tables: document pages 25–32 (PDF pages 39–46). The model guides education; state law, credentialing, and medical direction determine authorization.

AEMT scope examples in the current bank: supraglottic airway placement and peripheral IV initiation. Electrical cardioversion, manual defibrillation, ECG interpretation, and endotracheal intubation are not presented as EMT/AEMT procedures. Recognition, BLS support, and requests for higher-level care may be taught at these levels.

The national model defines EMR, EMT, AEMT, and Paramedic; Critical Care Paramedic is not a fifth national scope level. Critical-care content builds on the paramedic framework and does not imply authorization for transport ventilators, infusions, or other specialty procedures. State rules, employer credentialing, training, transport protocols, and medical direction govern practice.

Clinical hierarchy:
- AHA 2025 peer-reviewed BLS/ACLS/PALS guidelines for resuscitation; ALS recommendations must be translated into the learner's authorized actions.
- NAEMT PHTLS and GEMS topic frameworks for trauma and older-adult assessment. Public course pages describe the frameworks, not the full clinical curricula. Do not claim NAEMT endorsement or copy proprietary course questions.
- Accessible peer-reviewed clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, and original studies for specific claims; distinguish their evidence strength from the national consensus scope model.

## What Comes First?

Twelve original sequencing challenges: two EMT, two AEMT, and eight Paramedic. Cases are filtered to the selected track. Each stores stable step IDs, explicit priority dependencies, explanations, a parallel-task note, sources, scope reference, and check date. The reference sequence is a teaching model, not a full protocol; correctness is evaluated against dependency links. Changing states and failed-treatment conditions are explicit in the scenario. Confidence is chosen before feedback; attempts and drafts are browser-local and isolated from exam/flashcard scores.

- AHA 2025 Adult BLS and Pediatric BLS (links above): adult emergency activation/CPR/AED and infant choking transitions.
- AHA 2025 Adult ALS and tachyarrhythmia algorithm (links above): one VF shock cycle and rhythm-related unstable regular narrow-complex tachycardia.

AEMT airway escalation is conditional on persistently ineffective ventilation after BVM optimization and local SGA authorization. The hypoglycemia case specifies supported airway/breathing, unsafe oral swallowing, confirmed severe hypoglycemia, and prompt IV access under an IV-dextrose protocol. These cases do not authorize EMT use of SGA placement or IV dextrose.

## Build My Study Plan

A subject-independent browser-local planning tool. Learners supply topics, priorities, exam/start dates, available weekdays, daily minutes, and block length. The original scheduling heuristic seeks one preparation block and two active reviews on separate selected days per topic, places higher priorities earlier, ends before the exam, and reserves five-minute breaks between blocks. It warns when time or spacing cannot accommodate the three-block goal. The first review is eligible on the next selected study day; final reviews are reserved toward the end of the available schedule, allowing enough closing-day slots for the topics. Capacity and coverage can shift the actual dates. These intervals are practical planning choices, not personalized memory estimates or a validated readiness prediction.

Educational references checked October 1, 2026:
- Cornell Learning Strategies Center, Five-Day Study Plan: https://lsc.cornell.edu/how-to-study/studying-for-and-taking-exams/the-five-day-study-plan/
- Cornell Learning Strategies Center, Effective Study Strategies: https://lsc.cornell.edu/how-to-study/studying-for-and-taking-exams/effective-study-strategies/

The planner adapts the preparation/review and spaced-practice principles rather than copying the university's chart or claiming endorsement. It contains no new clinical guidance. Editing preserves date availability, daily capacity, preparation-before-review, and separate-day practice. Drafts and completion persist locally; print output shows the current plan. ICS export includes unfinished blocks grouped into all-day reminders and an exam-date reminder, with date-only values, escaped text, folded UTF-8 lines, and stable event UIDs. It downloads a file and does not access calendar accounts. Offline cache v16 includes the planner.

Study-plan instruction update: every activity now includes three concrete steps with suggested time allocations totaling the block duration. Preparation identifies key ideas, creates an explanation with the source closed, then checks it; review retrieves, checks, and retrieves missed ideas again; practice selects questions, answers/checks, and explains/retries mistakes; teach-back explains, verifies, and revises. The same directions appear in the plan, print view, and ICS descriptions, including existing saved plans. The 30/40/remainder time split is an original practical suggestion, not a research-derived optimal ratio. Five-minute breaks are shown explicitly between daily blocks.

11. **Prehospital rescue treatment of cluster epileptic seizures and status epilepticus** (January 2026), *Neurological Sciences* 47:100: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41486219/ . The 5-minute operational threshold is described in the peer-reviewed clinical update at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10295829/ . Older sources are retained where they directly support a specific detail.

**Selection policy:** NIH/NLM PubMed and PMC are discovery/indexing/archive platforms; an NIH-hosted article is not an NIH endorsement. Prioritize recent relevant peer-reviewed clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, and emergency-medicine publications; identify study design and retain older foundational evidence when a newer paper does not support the specific teaching point. Searches also checked JSTOR for paramedic/resuscitation and recent anaphylaxis material. The retrieved JSTOR material was historical or not suitable for these treatment decisions; no current treatment claim is attributed to an unverified JSTOR paper. A 2026 ILCOR CoSTR preprint and draft web recommendations were found but are not substituted for finalized peer-reviewed recommendations in this expansion.

**Teaching boundaries:** Original cases isolate a decision, not a full protocol. No new dosing recipe, RSI regimen, or uncredentialed intervention is supplied. Endotracheal intubation and ALS arrest medications are labeled for Paramedic practice under the supplied national scope framework; state rules, local protocols, credentialing and medical direction remain controlling. The airway finding case explicitly has a pulse so an absent trace is not casually excused as low arrest perfusion. Status cases specify normal glucose and ongoing convulsions, with no prior benzodiazepine; routes and repeat/escalation doses are protocol-dependent. The PEA ordering case uses two independent branches: a scheduled rhythm check can occur before epinephrine when access is delayed, so that valid ordering is accepted. Airway support, transport and other team tasks remain concurrent.
